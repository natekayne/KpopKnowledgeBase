# Third Generation of K-Pop
## Overview

Third-generation K-Pop is where the genre went from having a strong international fanbase to becoming a major global music industry. This era is usually connected to groups that debuted in the early to mid-2010s. Social media, YouTube, streaming, and international fandoms became way more important during this generation, which made it much easier for fans outside Korea to follow releases, performances, and variety content in real time.

Physical album sales also started getting much bigger again because fandoms became more organized and collecting different album versions, photocards, and other inclusions became a huge part of K-Pop culture.
## Best-Selling and Major Groups

- **BTS** – Became the biggest global breakthrough of third-gen and one of the best-selling Korean acts ever. Their success in the United States and other major music markets completely changed how the industry viewed international promotion. 
- **EXO** – One of the biggest album-selling groups of the generation and helped bring back million-selling physical albums in K-Pop. 
- **SEVENTEEN** – Became one of K-Pop's strongest physical album sellers and are especially known for their performance, synchronization, and involvement in their own music.
- **TWICE** – One of the most successful girl groups of the generation, with major sales and huge popularity in both Korea and Japan.
- **BLACKPINK** – Became one of the biggest girl groups in the world and helped expand K-Pop even further into Western markets.
- **Red Velvet** – Known for switching between brighter "Red" concepts and darker "Velvet" concepts while building a strong discography.
- **GOT7** – Built a large international fanbase and became especially popular outside Korea.
## Why This Generation Matters

Third gen changed the scale of K-Pop. The [[second-generation]] proved that K-Pop could succeed internationally, but third-gen groups pushed that success into major Western charts, stadium tours, award shows, and mainstream media. BTS reaching No. 1 on major U.S. charts and BLACKPINK performing at major international festivals showed how much the industry had changed.
### Connection to the Next Generation

The [[fourth-generation]] inherited a much bigger worldwide audience, stronger streaming culture, and even more intense album collecting. Third gen was probably the biggest turning point in the [[global-expansion]] of K-Pop because this is when it stopped feeling like a niche international genre and became part of mainstream pop culture.

> Third-generation K-Pop was the era that proved Korean idol groups could compete at the highest level of the global music industry.
